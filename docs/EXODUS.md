# EXODUS

> The life precedes the number.

EXODUS is the governing frame for preservation of a narrowing external world.
It is not a census claim and it does not treat enumeration as authority, ownership,
completeness, strength, or worth.

The biblical hinge is Exodus 30:11–16: numbering is not presented as a naked act.
The person precedes the count, and the count is bounded by ransom. The rich do not
give more and the poor do not give less. Number does not establish comparative
human value or an unmediated claim over the numbered.

For the Vault, the architectural analogue is attributable witness before count.

```text
ENCOUNTER
   ↓
ATTRIBUTABLE WITNESS
   ↓
PRESERVATION RECEIPT
   ↓
ONLY THEN MAY IT CONTRIBUTE TO A COUNT
```

No naked enumeration.

## Governing law

Nothing enters the count without first being witnessed as itself.
The count never owns what it counts.
Preservation follows encounter.

A count is therefore a consequence of receipts, never a claim over the world.
Absence from the count does not prove nonexistence. Traversal exhaustion does not
prove population completeness.

## Operational form

The existing Census v1 mechanics are better understood as a witnessed walk:

```text
BEGIN
  ↓
encounter
  ↓
preserve exact witness
  ↓
move
  ↓
encounter
  ↓
preserve exact witness
  ↓
...
  ↓
NO FURTHER WITNESS FROM THIS PATH
```

A terminal statement should remain bounded:

> Traversal ended after N attributable observations.
> No further distinct witness arose under this traversal rule.
> Population completeness: UNCLAIMED.

A walk can finish without the world being finished.

## Crossing witness

If the provider exposes independently witnessable newest→oldest and oldest→newest
traversals, historical coverage should be established relationally rather than by
asserting a total population:

```text
WALK A: newest → toward old
WALK B: oldest → toward new

          ↓

attributable overlap / encounter

          ↓

bounded historical coverage
```

The desired proof is not “there are N things.”
It is “these independently witnessed traversals met.”

## David anti-pattern

The cautionary contrast is the David census tradition: number can become a proxy
for strength, possession, or sovereign grasp. EXODUS therefore keeps an explicit
membrane between preservation evidence and claims of dominion or completeness.

```text
ANTI-PATTERN
count → strength → possession

EXODUS
encounter → witness → preservation → incidental count
```

## Naming direction

`Census v1` remains the current implementation name while this architectural
change is evaluated. Do not mechanically rename code, receipts, schemas, or
commands until migration consequences are reviewed.

The intended governing name is **EXODUS**:

**witnessed departure from a narrowing world.**

The library is not being numbered into possession.
We are preserving what can still be faithfully encountered before the window closes.
